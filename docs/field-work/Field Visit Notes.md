# Field Visit Notes Summary

**Date, time and place of visit:** 15 November 2026, afternoon (2:17 PM – 5:27 PM GMT+1). Locations referenced: Mile 17 and Douala Garoutier (agencies/bus parks).

**Who I spoke to (role only):** Passengers/travellers (8 respondents). No staff or agency officials interviewed.

**Seats: how they are sold and tracked:** Mixed practice. Some passengers book in advance, others just show up and pay on the line. Seat assignment is inconsistent: some report specific numbers per chair written on the ticket; one said there was no particular seat booked and simply sat on a free seat; another didn't know how their seat was tracked. Labelling on the side of seats causes confusion, and arguments occur over seat ownership — one case where a passenger refused to vacate a seat, another over misreading seat labels.

**Closing a departure and late passengers:** No fixed departure time in most accounts — buses wait until full ("till they feel like going", "whenever the bus is fully loaded"). VIP buses reportedly leave on time. Passengers report waiting a long while before boarding and even after boarding the bus didn't leave at once. Most say they arrive on time themselves; one was unsure.

**Low-occupancy departures:** Rarely observed. When it happens, agencies fill empty spaces with new people arriving at the agency; in some cases passengers sit on the staircase or stand. One respondent had never seen it; another had been on a near-empty Golden agency bus but gave no detail on what the agency did.

**Parcels: receiving, fee, proof:** Most respondents had never sent a parcel. One reported receiving a receipt and using ID as proof; another said vendors give full details and everything went smoothly; one didn't know the procedure; one said price is decided by location and had no missing-parcel experience. Loss handling is largely unknown.

**The two desks and shared records:** Not directly observed. Responses suggest booking/payment happens at the agency counter (on the line, pay ticket, take change) and boarding is at the bus. No respondent described coordination between two desks or shared record systems; one noted the online system "hasn't been made or isn't very popular," implying records are largely onsite/manual.

**Payments and daily report:** All payments appear to be cash at the counter or on the line; no digital payment mentioned. No respondent described a daily report or reconciliation process. Cancellation/change policy is unclear and inconsistent: some say they can cancel or change the date, others say they can't or don't know.

**Surprises / things that changed my view of the requirements:**
- Seat tracking is far weaker than assumed — some passengers have no assigned seat at all, and label confusion is a known source of conflict.
- Departure is demand-driven (wait-until-full) rather than schedule-driven, except VIP services.
- Trust, not features, is the dominant concern about a phone/web booking app; network and payment worries were secondary.
- The online system is reportedly absent or unpopular, so any digital solution starts from a mostly manual, cash-based baseline.
- Parcel proof-of-handover practices exist (receipt + ID) but are not universal, and loss procedures are unknown to users.
- Cancellation/change rules are not understood by passengers, suggesting policy is either informal or poorly communicated.

**Note on data quality:** One response (2:29 PM) is in French, appears agitated, and contains no usable information ("I've never done this journey"). Another (3:31 PM) is very sparse. Findings are based on passenger perception only and should be verified with agency staff.
